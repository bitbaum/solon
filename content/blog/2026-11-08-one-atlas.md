Until this week Solon had two maps. The home page had a globe that showed whose law Solon holds. Places had a flat map of Switzerland coloured by tax, with long lists of cantons and communes underneath. They looked alike, but they behaved differently, they showed different things, and neither led to the other.

Now there is one atlas.

## The world, then a country, then a place

You start with the Earth. Every country Solon holds law for is shaded by how much of it is here, with a dot so the small ones (Liechtenstein is 160 km²) can be found and tapped.

Tap a country and the globe turns and zooms to it. Its places are drawn on the same map: Switzerland's 2,110 communes, coloured by what tax would cost you in each at the income you enter. Tap a commune and its card opens: the tax there, how it ranks, and a button to put it beside others in a comparison. Beside the map, the panel shows what Solon holds for the country: how many of its laws, word for word, with a link to read them and propose a change; its places; and, folded away, where every figure comes from.

The address follows you. `/places?in=ee` opens Estonia, so a view can be shared as a link.

## Fewer things to scroll

The long rows of buttons ("Canton (26)", "Municipality (2,110)") and the full list of sources under the map are gone from the page. You find a place by tapping it, or by typing its name, a postcode or an address into the one search box. The sources are one tap away under "Where this comes from". If you'd rather read than look, the map turns into a list.

## One renderer

Both old maps already drew with the same piece of code, the fleet's map package, which is a globe when you are far away and a flat map when you are close. So the atlas is not a third map. It is that renderer used once, the way it was built to be used, and the two components that used it separately are deleted.

## What is drawn, and what is next

Switzerland's communes are drawn today, and Liechtenstein's eleven arrive from the same Swiss boundary file. Estonia's 15 counties and 78 municipalities are in Places, with its income tax, but are not drawn yet. Next, their outlines come from Eurostat's boundaries of every municipality in the EU, the same source that will draw Austria and Germany. After that, Wyoming's counties, and the steps in between a country and its communes: cantons, counties, states.
