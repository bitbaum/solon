# Townsism

**The world should be made of towns that govern themselves.**

Countries and their subdivisions are the middle layer. They draw lines from the center, assign powers from the center, and redraw them when the center finds it convenient. A town is the scale at which a person can know who decides, see the money, and leave if the place goes bad.

This is the SSOT narrative for Townsism. Everything else — Mightville, Deyville, Solon, OrangeCat, the fictional map — quotes this. It does not replace the law of any place you already live.

_Written 2026-08-15. Status: the keystone essay. Not a legal filing. Not a product pitch._

---

## 1 · One sentence

Power belongs at the smallest scale that can actually hold it — the town — and every larger layer exists only by the towns' consent, with exit always open.

## 2 · The claim

A **town** is small enough to be legible and large enough to feed, house, and judge itself. People know each other, or can. The treasury is a number a resident can read. The law is a list a resident can finish. The vote is a signature a resident can recount.

A **country**, and the provinces / cantons / oblasts / Länder / régions it carves, is a different kind of thing:

- **Imposed from above.** The center draws the border and names the powers. The people inside did not choose the line.
- **Unstable by design.** France merged 22 metropolitan régions into 13 in 2016 by decree. A border the capital can move was never yours.
- **Powerless where it matters.** Most subdivisions administer. They do not govern. Tax, spend, legislate — the center keeps a veto, or keeps the power outright.

The town is not a smaller country. It is the unit that does not need a center to be real.

This is not a new idea. City-states did it. The Hanseatic League did it. Singapore does a version of it. Charter cities, network states, seasteading, and panarchy are all reaching for the same shape. Townsism's addition is not another theory. It is the insistence that the shape is a **town**, and that the town must actually run — money, law, vote, hospitality — or the theory is literature.

## 3 · Tenets

1. **Federalism-maximalism.** Push every decision down until it cannot go further without breaking. Federate only for what a town cannot do alone: defense of last resort, shared standards, large infrastructure. Federation is a contract between towns, not a parent.
2. **Right of exit.** A person can leave a town. A town can leave a federation. A rule that cannot be walked away from is not a rule — it is a cage. Exit is the check that voting alone has never been.
3. **Open-source by default.** The software a town runs, and the hardware it can, must be inspectable. A government you cannot read is a government you cannot verify.
4. **Hospitality (the Ossetian rule).** Peace between towns. Free trade. Free movement. The guest is sacred. The host's town is the host's town — respect it, or leave. These are not opposites. They are the same ethic from two sides of the door.
5. **Against concentrated veto — state or corporation.** A ministry that can override a town and a platform that can deplatform a town are the same shape. Townsism is not "smaller government, bigger companies." It is local legitimacy, cryptographic verification, and no silent veto from a distant office.

## 4 · What we are not saying

**We do not tell you to break the law of the place you live.** The real state will not honor a slider. Tax that is owed is owed. Townsism is not a tax shelter and not a secession kit you download.

**Software is not sovereignty.** Solon can give a town a treasury you can audit, votes you can recount, and a law you can read. It cannot make a neighboring army go home, and it cannot make a capital cede land. Recognition is a political fact. The software is how a community becomes worth recognizing.

**"Abolish countries" is a mood. "Make the middle layer obsolete" is the work.** A town that runs itself honestly, pays what it legally owes, and offers a life people choose will do more to retire the middle layer than any slogan. We prefer the register that is harder to argue against: outgrow, route around, make unnecessary.

**Virtual first, physical when earned.** The first towns can be communities on the rails — real members, real treasuries, real votes — without waiting for a border to move. Physical territory is the north star, not the blocker.

## 5 · How a town actually runs

A self-governing town needs four organs. They already have names in this stack:

| Organ | What it does | What we are building |
|---|---|---|
| Economy | Move value without asking a bank | **OrangeCat** — Bitcoin / Lightning, maker profiles, pay links |
| Capability | Get work done without an employer | **FleetCrown** — an AI fleet a town (or a person) can run |
| Governance | Decide without a silent veto | **Solon** — Bitcoin-signed votes, watch-only treasury, append-only audit |
| Belonging | A why people will actually join | **Townsism** (this essay) → **Mightville** (the movement) → **Deyville** (the OS a town runs) |

The Cat proposes; Solon disposes. Money-movement without a legislature becomes rule-by-algorithm. A legislature without money-movement is a forum. Both, plus exit, is a polity.

Compelled tax and chosen contribution must never be merged. The reserve is what your real jurisdiction requires. The contribution is what you freely give — to a person, a public good, a town. Honesty is the product.

## 6 · How to start without asking permission

Do the legal thing that is already a town in miniature:

- a Swiss Verein or cooperative with a public treasury
- a co-living house that votes on its own rent and rules
- an HOA or village that is tired of opaque books
- a virtual town with a real membership roll and a watch-only Bitcoin treasury

Run the books where anyone can see them. Sign the votes. Publish the decisions. Keep the guest sacred and the door yours. That is Townsism this quarter. "Sovereign Deyville" is the name of the destination, not the next ticket.

## 7 · What success looks like

Not a follower count. Not a flag.

A stranger can open a town's books and finish them. A member can recount last month's vote with the published signatures. A guest can arrive and be fed. A dissenter can leave with their keys and their name. A neighboring town can trade without a ministry in the middle.

When that is ordinary, the middle layer will have less to do. That is the whole dream.
